# ISS Medical Checklist — Emergency Procedures (1.101–1.108)

> Fuente: https://www.governmentattic.org/19docs/NASA-ISSmedicalEmergManual_2016.pdf

Extract of the NASA Johnson Space Center *Emergency Medical Procedures Manual for the International Space Station* (SODF: MED CL: EMERGENCY, revisions of March–June 2015), released in full under FOIA case 16-JSC-F-00174 (14 March 2016). Work of the U.S. Government. The text below keeps the order and wording of the steps; figures, part numbers and drug doses for injectable medications are left out on purpose (the onboard checklist and the flight surgeon govern those).

Context from NASA (Hailey et al., *Evaluating the Medical Kit System for the ISS*, 2011, NTRS 20110010924): of 6 ISS crewmembers, 4 are trained as Crew Medical Officers (CMO), typically with a technical/engineering background and approximately 25 hours of training. CMO duties in flight are augmented by communication with the medical support team (video conference, email, phone) and by the ISS Medical Checklist, which provides "directive steps in support of routine activities and medical intervention". Emergency medication and hardware live in the **Emergency Medical Treatment Pack (Red)**; the kit also includes a defibrillator (AED), a Respiratory Support Pack (oxygen, AMBU bag), the Crew Medical Restraint System, and the Medical Diagnostic Pack (Blue: blood pressure monitor, blood oximeter, stethoscope).

Recurring step in every procedure: **"Call MCC-H to request PMC"** — call Mission Control Houston to request a Private Medical Conference with the flight surgeon.

## 1.101 Severe allergic reaction

Objective: to rapidly treat a severe allergic reaction.

1. Deploy and use epinephrine (EpiPen) from the Emergency Medical Treatment Pack (Red): remove it from its tube, form a fist around it with the thumb closest to the safety cap, pull off the safety cap. Do not put fingers over the needle end; do not give the shot through clothing.
2. Using a jabbing motion, press the needle end perpendicular to and firmly against the patient's outer thigh until a click is heard or felt. Hold firmly for 10 seconds.
3. Discard the used EpiPen in its tube, secured with gray tape. **Call MCC-H to request PMC.**
4. Epinephrine may be repeated every 20 minutes if symptoms recur or progress.
5. Go to 2.0.403 ALLERGIC REACTION (exams, procedures and treatment).

## 1.102 AED assisted CPR

1. **Assess**: assess environment for safety hazards. Assess patient status.
   - Patient unresponsive and breathing normally → relocate to and restrain patient on the Crew Medical Restraint System; page additional crewmember for assistance; call MCC-H to request PMC; go to the respiratory assessment (1.103, block 13).
   - Patient unresponsive and not breathing, or no normal breathing (only gasping) → perform CPR.
2. **Perform CPR**: 30 chest compressions for every 2 breaths (1 second each breath) for 5 cycles (approximately 2 minutes). As required, relocate to and restrain the patient on the Crew Medical Restraint System; page an additional crewmember; call MCC-H to request PMC.
3. **Deploy AED**: connect AED electrodes to the patient's chest; press AED ON (green); follow the verbal prompts (read them on screen if inaudible).
4. **Monitor patient response**: after the AED prompts "Start CPR", perform a 10-second pulse check.
   - Pulse detected or spontaneous breathing → stop chest compressions, assist breathing as necessary, go to the respiratory assessment.
   - No pulse → 30 compressions for every 2 breaths until prompted by the AED; repeat after each "Start CPR" prompt.
5. If two rescuers are available: rescuer 1 deploys the AED, rescuer 2 goes to the ALS algorithm (1.103).

## 1.103 ALS algorithm (advanced life support, second rescuer)

Intraosseous access, emergency medications from the ALS Medication Kit given according to AED rhythm analysis, then:

- **CPR termination**: coordinate with the surgeon, if available. Consider termination only if all apply: at least 10 minutes of CPR since the last dose of medication, no pulse or spontaneous breathing, and at least 45 minutes elapsed since the start of resuscitation.
- **Respiratory assessment** (block 13): is the patient awake and able to speak? If not, are lips, face or hands blue/gray, breathing rate < 12 or > 30 per minute, or the patient snoring or having difficulty inhaling?
  - Yes to any → assist breathing (mouth to mouth or AMBU bag, one breath every 5 seconds) and provide oxygen from the Respiratory Support Pack.
  - No to all → provide oxygen with a low-flow non-rebreather mask.
- Lung assessment with stethoscope (breath sounds on both sides), IV fluids, attach monitoring equipment (blood pressure/ECG, blood oximeter), **call MCC-H to request PMC**.

## 1.105 Choking

Objective: to rapidly clear airway obstruction.

1. Check patient response: is the patient awake?
2. If awake → perform abdominal thrusts: position behind the patient, make a fist, place the fist above the belly button, pull sharply inward and upward; repeat until the object clears or the patient becomes unconscious.
3. If the patient becomes unconscious → open the airway (head tilt–chin lift), inspect for a foreign object and remove it if easily accessible, pinch the nose and attempt to give breaths, then go to 1.102 AED assisted CPR.
4. When the object clears → obtain vital signs (pulse, respiratory rate, blood pressure) and record them for the surgeon.
5. Consult surgeon: **call MCC-H to request PMC.**

## 1.106 Toxic exposure

Objective: to decontaminate and treat after toxic exposure.

1. **Rescuer safety**: to minimize the number of crew exposed, take respiratory, eye and skin precautions before removing the patient from the exposure.
2. **Decontaminate**: skin — quickly remove soiled clothes, brush off particulate, wipe liquids, irrigate with water. Eyes — remove contact lenses; immediately flush with water from a drink bag, then irrigate at the eyewash station until pain resolves or the surgeon says to stop. Respiratory — if difficulty breathing, perform 1.108.
3. **Exam**: call MCC-H to request PMC. **If comm is unavailable**: eye exam for eye exposures, or the sick-call exam for all other exposures.
4. Obtain history (chemicals, medications, other new exposures), photo-document affected body parts and downlink the photos for the surgeon.

## 1.107 Seizure

Objective: to give medication to suppress seizure activity. Seizures cause loss of consciousness and uncontrolled muscle contraction.

1. **Protect from injury**: prevent impact of the body with hard structures; attempt to locate the patient in the center of a module away from structures. Never attempt to pry open or put anything in the mouth during a seizure. Seizures typically last less than a minute.
2. CMO gives the anticonvulsant intramuscular injection from the Topical & Injectable Medication Pack (Brown) as the checklist describes; if the seizure continues for more than 5 minutes, or for repeated seizures, the dose is repeated in 10 minutes. Steps 2 and 3 can be done in parallel if several CMOs are available.
3. Apply the blood oximeter and record SpO2; administer oxygen (non-rebreather mask); obtain IV access.
4. Targeted physical exam: after a seizure the patient will be confused for several minutes; examine head, extremities, teeth and tongue for injuries; photo-document injuries.
5. **Call MCC-H to request PMC**; oral medication afterwards only as directed by the surgeon. Collect history of the event.

## 1.108 Difficulty breathing

Objective: to evaluate and start treatment for difficulty breathing.

1. **Start treatment**: count pulse and respiratory rate (count for 15 seconds and multiply by four) and record them for the surgeon. Administer oxygen (Respiratory Support Pack, low-flow non-rebreather mask). Administer inhaled albuterol (two puffs, holding the breath 8 to 10 seconds).
2. Apply the blood oximeter and record SpO2.
3. Airway exam: inspect mouth, nose, neck and chest for changes in normal anatomy and increased secretions; look for increased work of breathing. Listen to the lungs on both sides, front and back.
4. Obtain blood pressure and pulse.
5. Obtain history: events related to the symptoms, medications, fever and cough, severity. **Call MCC-H to request PMC.**
